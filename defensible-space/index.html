Drone inspections of defensible space usually mean a person scrolling through hundreds of photos. This page does the first pass automatically, from the same 77-photo survey used in Splat to Earth. It needs no building footprints and no trained imagery model. Each flagged structure comes with the source photo that shows it best, with the detected footprint drawn on it.

Why the heights don't come from the splat

I started by measuring straight off the Gaussian splat. It doesn't work. Over this park, 70% of the splat's surface sits more than 2.5 m above the stereo surface: floaters that reproduce the grass colour at the wrong depth. At independent ground checkpoints the splat reads a median 8.5 m high against USGS 3DEP lidar (19 m RMSE). COLMAP's multi-view stereo, from the same photos, reads +0.48 m (0.72 m RMSE). Starting the splat from the stereo cloud halves its error, but that's still metres. A splat is a great way to look at a site and a risky way to measure one. The splat page has an audit layer that shows exactly where.

How it works

A COLMAP multi-view-stereo cloud, already in the georeferenced frame, is gridded at 0.5 m into a surface model and a colour image. Ground comes from USGS 3DEP lidar, shifted into the survey's vertical frame by the bias measured on the splat page, with a smooth local correction capped at ±2 m. Height above ground plus a greenness index sort each cell into six classes: structure, tree canopy, shrub / tall grass, low vegetation, raised object and ground.

A structure is a flat, non-green component of at least 30 m² and at least 3 m wide that stands above its own surroundings. That last test, against the ground right next to it rather than the lidar, removed a raised footpath that otherwise looked like a building. Small objects that only look green at their edges (picnic tables, bins) become "raised objects": in Zone 0 those are worth a look for combustibles.

The ring metrics also say how much of each ring the drone actually saw. "Not observed" isn't the same as "clear", and a screening tool should say so.

Here it finds the one building in the park, the shelter, and rates it watch: the wooden picnic tables sit in Zone 0, and a band of tall prairie grass reaches into Zone 1. That is what the photo shows.

This is a screening heuristic for deciding which properties a person should inspect first. It is not a compliance determination. The thresholds are parameters, the classes come from colour and geometry rather than a trained model, and the drone data here is a public park in Ohio, not a wildland-urban interface. The same pipeline is how I'd triage parcels for an insurer, a fire-safe council or a utility.

Output

A GeoJSON with one footprint per structure and one ring polygon per zone, each carrying its metrics, ready for ArcGIS Pro, ArcGIS Online or QGIS. There's also a north-up classification PNG, a JSON summary and a photo chip per structure.

georef-splat zones --points fused.ply --manifest georeference_manifest.json \
    --ground 3dep --ground-offset -1.27 --crop-to-cameras 25 --out zones/
